Case Brief

What happened at Foxglove Manor

อ่านเรื่องราวการค้นพบศพ — คลิกคำที่ขีดเส้นใต้เพื่อดูคำแปล

The rain began just after two o'clock and never let up. By mid-afternoon, Foxglove Manor sat behind a grey curtain of water, its tall windows rattling with every gust of wind.

Lord Harrington, a wealthy art collector, had spent the morning in unusually good spirits. He told his housekeeper, Mrs. Pratt, that he was expecting "three old friends" that afternoon — though anyone who knew Lord Harrington understood that "friend" was a generous word for a man who trusted almost no one with his money, his paintings, or his opinions.

At 4:15 PM, Mrs. Pratt carried a tray of tea down the long hallway toward the private study, only to find the door ajar and Lord Harrington slumped over his desk, motionless. She dropped the tray and screamed for the telephone.

By 4:30 PM, Detective Vance stood in the doorway of the study, rain still dripping from the hem of her coat. The room told its own story: overturned books, a shattered vase, and Lord Harrington's most treasured painting gone from the wall above the fireplace.

The medical examiner was quick with his estimate: Lord Harrington had been killed sometime between 3:00 PM and 4:00 PM — squarely in the middle of the afternoon's heaviest downpour.

Detective Vance opened her notebook. According to Mrs. Pratt, exactly three visitors had come to Foxglove Manor that day, each one walking the same long hallway of polished hardwood floors to reach the study door. Three different reasons. Three different stories. Detective Vance settled into a chair by the fire and began, one by one, to hear them out.

Three visitors. Three umbrellas. Three stories that don't quite match. The hallway remembers more than any of them think, Detective. Who really walked through that door — and who is lying about it?

Interview Room

Three visitors, three stories

อ่านคำให้การของผู้ต้องสงสัยทั้งสามคนอย่างละเอียด — เวลาและรายละเอียดเล็กๆ สำคัญมาก

AP

Arthur Pendelton

FINANCIAL ADVISOR
Arrived 2:15 PMTo Garden Room 2:50 PM

Detective Vance's pen hovered over her notebook as the first visitor took a seat, straightening his tie before he spoke a word.

"I arrived at 2:15 PM, well before the rain started. Lord Harrington and I talked about his money until 2:50 PM. Then I went to the glass garden room to read my documents. I stayed there the whole time and didn't hear anything until the police arrived."

Detective Vance made a small note beside his name and gestured for the next visitor to come in.

BV

Beatrice Vance

ART RESTORER
Arrived 3:15 PMLeft the study ~3:25 PM

The second visitor arrived still wrapped in a damp coat, her fingertips faintly stained with paint.

"I drove through the heavy rain and arrived at 3:15 PM. As soon as I entered the main door, I put my dripping wet umbrella into the stand in the entrance hall. I immediately walked down the long hallway to the study to deliver a painting. Lord Harrington was alive when I left ten minutes later."

Detective Vance tapped her pen twice against the notebook — a small habit of hers whenever a detail seemed a little too tidy — and thanked her.

CM

Charles Montgomery

ARCHITECT
Arrived 3:45 PMLeft 4:00 PM

The last visitor entered looking, if anything, the most soaked of the three, his coat sleeves dark with rain.

"I arrived at 3:45 PM while it was raining very heavily outside. I was soaked when I reached the door. My umbrella is a very expensive antique, so I didn't want to leave it at the front door. Therefore, I kept it folded, held it pointing down by my side, and walked straight down the long hardwood hallway to his study. I left at 4:00 PM."

Detective Vance closed her notebook slowly and looked toward the long hallway outside the study door, now quiet except for the rain against the windows.

🔎 Detective Vance's Notebook

  • Does everyone's story fit inside the murder window of 3:00–4:00 PM?
